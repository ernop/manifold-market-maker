# SF Air Quality Significantly improves in 2024

## Finding

The city scorecard page is gone (its URL redirects to the general scorecards index), so the best substitute is its own data source: the EPA daily AQI from the single Bay Area Air District monitor at 10 Arkansas Street. EPA's current summary gives San Francisco County 254 Good days out of 334 days with an AQI in 2024, but EPA now applies the May 2024 PM2.5 breakpoints (Good at or below 9.0 µg/m³) to every year, which is the redefinition the market excludes. I recounted each day with the old PM2.5 breakpoint (Good at or below 12.0 µg/m³) and the unchanged breakpoints for ozone, NO2, CO and PM10. That recount reproduces the scorecard's published history (2016: 311 vs 310, 2017: 276 vs 276, 2019: 313 vs 313, 2020: 277 vs 277, 2021: 311 vs 310) and gives 303 Good days and 31 Moderate days for 2024, short of the more than 320 required. The monitor had no valid daily value on 32 days of 2024; 17 of those had partial hourly readings that would count as Good, which brings the total to exactly 320, still not more than 320.

## Evidence

- [EPA AirData pre-generated files](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/download_files.html) (files regenerated June 2026, accessed 2026-09-29): `annual_aqi_by_county_2024.zip` lists San Francisco County 2024 with 334 days with AQI, 254 Good, 80 Moderate, none worse, and PM2.5 as the defining pollutant on 304 days.
- [EPA: Do all AQI plots use the most current AQI breakpoints?](https://www.epa.gov/outdoor-air-quality-data/do-all-aqi-plots-use-most-current-aqi-breakpoints-regardless-which-year) (accessed 2026-09-29): "every AQI summary from AirData is based on the current AQI breakpoints, regardless of year." This is why EPA's current 2019-2021 counts (251, 227, 255) are lower than the scorecard's 313, 277, 310.
- [EPA fact sheet: Final Updates to the AQI for Particulate Matter](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-02/pm-naaqs-air-quality-index-fact-sheet.pdf) (February 2024): the PM2.5 Good range changed from 0.0-12.0 to 0.0-9.0 µg/m³, effective May 6, 2024; breakpoints for other pollutants did not change.
- [EPA AirData daily PM2.5 file, daily_88101_2024.zip](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/daily_88101_2024.zip) (accessed 2026-09-29): for the San Francisco monitor, the AQI EPA reports matches only the new breakpoints on 332 of 334 days (the other 2 match both), including January-April 2024, so the whole year is on the new scale. The file shows 31 days with a 24-hour average above 12.0 µg/m³, for example October 2-4 and December 7-8, 2024.
- [Recount from the EPA AirData daily files](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/download_files.html) (my calculation, 2026-09-29): daily PM2.5, ozone, NO2, CO and PM10 files filtered to San Francisco County; PM2.5 AQI recomputed from the 24-hour average (truncated to 0.1 µg/m³) under both scales; daily AQI is the maximum across pollutants; Good means 50 or less. Good days on the new scale / old scale: 2019 251/313, 2020 227/277, 2021 256/311, 2022 260/313, 2023 299/344, 2024 254/303. The new-scale figures match EPA's published counts to within one day, and the old-scale figures match the scorecard's published values to within one day.
- [EPA AirData ozone file, daily_44201_2024.zip](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/daily_44201_2024.zip) and [NO2 file, daily_42602_2024.zip](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/daily_42602_2024.zip) (accessed 2026-09-29): San Francisco ozone and NO2 data exist only for January 1 to February 29, 2024; PM2.5 is the only pollutant reported for the rest of the year.
- [Archived scorecard page, "Days with an EPA Air Quality Index Rating of Good"](https://web.archive.org/web/20231005072108/https://sfgov.org/scorecards/environment/days-epa-air-quality-index-rating-good) (October 2023 snapshot): the measure is the EPA AQI from the single Arkansas Street monitor, and the page points to EPA's AQI report for the counts.
- [SF Controller, Results for Fiscal Year 2017-18](https://sfcontroller.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Auditing/FY18%20Annual%20Performance%20Report%20FINAL.pdf) (2018): Good days fell from 310 to 276 in 2017; the old-scale recount gives 311 and 276.
- [Current scorecard URL](https://sfgov.org/scorecards/environment/days-epa-air-quality-index-rating-good) (accessed 2026-09-29): redirects to the general [SF Performance Scorecards](https://www.sf.gov/resource--2024--san-francisco-performance-scorecards) page; the replacement [Sustainability and Climate Action Scorecard](https://www.sf.gov/resource--2024--sustainability-and-climate-action-scorecard) is an embedded dashboard whose page text shows no air quality measure; I could not inspect the dashboard contents.
- [BAAQMD 2025 Air Monitoring Network Plan](https://www.baaqmd.gov/~/media/files/technical-services/air-monitoring-network-plans/2025_network_plan_draft-pdf.pdf) (2025): the San Francisco site at 10 Arkansas St is still listed with ozone, CO, NO2, PM10 and PM2.5 monitors, so the 2024 gaps are missing data, not a site closure.
- [EPA AirData metro-area file, annual_aqi_by_cbsa_2024.zip](https://aqs.epa.gov/aqsweb/airdata/annual_aqi_by_cbsa_2024.zip) (accessed 2026-09-29): the San Francisco-Oakland-Hayward metro area had 149 Good days of 366 on the new scale, so a regional substitute is also far below 320.

## Other side

The case for YES rests on the 32 days with no valid reading. On the measured days, 91% were Good on the old scale (303 of 334); applied to a full year that is about 332, above 320, and a higher share than 2019 (86%) or 2021 (85%). A YES holder can argue that monitor downtime is not pollution and that the market asks what the number would have been. This does not change the recommendation. The criterion counts days with a Good rating, and the scorecard always counted rated days; crediting every day with partial data still gives exactly 320, which fails "more than 320", and reaching 321 requires assuming conditions on at least one of 15 days with no measurement at all. Ozone and NO2 were also unmeasured from March to December 2024, and in earlier years they made 0 to 15 days a year non-Good, so a like-for-like count could be slightly below 303. On EPA's current definition the figure is 254, far below. For context on the title, 2024 was worse than 2023 on both scales (303 vs 344 old, 254 vs 299 new).

## Before resolving

Decide whether days with no monitor data should be estimated. If only days with an actual rating count, which is how the scorecard always worked, resolve NO. If you think the market should estimate a full year, the estimate is about 330 and would point to YES; I do not recommend that reading because even crediting every partial-data day stops at exactly 320.
