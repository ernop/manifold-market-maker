# Will Shawn Fain have started another major strike or labor dispute by mid 2026?

## Finding

From August 2024 Fain led the UAW's "Keep the Promise" campaign against Stellantis over its delayed Belvidere reopening and other investment promises from the 2023 contract. Grievances from 28 locals covered about 98% of the UAW's roughly 43,000 Stellantis members; on 2024-09-17 Fain announced strike authorization votes in a livestreamed speech, and on 2024-09-27 he urged every UAW worker at Stellantis to vote yes, after which Stellantis sued the union in federal courts in at least nine states. The campaign ran in Fain's signature style: profane videos (Tavares did a "piss-poor" job and "needs to go"), the ShitcanCarlos.com strike-pledge site showing the CEO in a garbage can, and plant rallies, with coverage from Reuters, CNN, and The Detroit News. Tavares resigned on 2024-12-01, and Stellantis recommitted to Belvidere and the Durango on 2025-01-22. That meets the description's tests (a dispute Fain started, 20,000+ workers, major coverage of his campaigning); no other 2024-2026 candidate does, and Fain was still president at the close, so the successor clause does not come into play.

## Other side

No strike happened. Only three locals (Los Angeles, Denver, and Rockford) passed strike votes, Fain's own Kokomo local voted no, and the UAW paused the drive and withdrew grievances by early November 2024, so a strict reader could say it never "involved" 20,000 workers in action. If "another major strike" means an actual walkout like 2023, the answer is NO: the 2024 UC strike was called by Local 4811, not Fain; the 2026 UC dispute ended in a contract without a strike; and the 2025-2026 walkouts and votes (GE Aerospace, about 600; Sterling Heights, about 6,000, called off) were far below 20,000. The 27% close reflects only 2 bettors. I still lean YES because the question says "strike or labor dispute," and a Fain-led national campaign with strike threats, lawsuits, and a CEO's exit is a major labor dispute.
